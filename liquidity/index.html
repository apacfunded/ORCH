Fill the pool first. Then open the doors.

A bonding curve is just two numbers: how much virtual liquidity it starts with, and where it stops. pump.fun hard-codes both. We hand them to you.

1

Set your opening cap

Choose where trading starts — $10K, $25K, $50K or a custom figure. Higher opening cap means less dilution for day-one buyers and a chart that doesn't start at zero.

2

Set the graduation cap

Pick the market cap where the curve completes and liquidity migrates to a locked AMM pool. Short curves graduate fast; long curves hold more SOL on the way up.

3

FAQ

Why would I want a higher starting market cap?

Because the $4K–$20K zone is where most coins get sniped, dumped and abandoned. Opening at $10K or $25K means every early buyer enters at a price closer to the real community, the chart doesn't start at a dot, and the pool holds real SOL from the first minute.

Doesn't a higher start just mean fewer tokens per SOL?

Yes — that's the trade. A higher opening cap means less upside from launch to graduation but a far smaller gap between first buyer and hundredth. You pick the balance; the builder shows the multiple before you deploy.

How is this different from just buying your own supply on pump.fun?

Bundling fills the curve with tokens that sit in your wallets and overhang the chart. Liquidity sets the curve's virtual reserves instead: nobody holds the "pre-fill", it's just math in the pool. No dev bags, no overhang, verifiable on-chain.

What happens at graduation?

The SOL collected on the curve plus the remaining supply migrate to an AMM pool and the LP is locked. From there it trades like any other Solana token.

What is $LIQ for?

$LIQ is the platform coin. A share of every launch fee and curve trading fee buys back $LIQ, and $LIQ holders get fee-free launches above a holding threshold. Details posted at launch.

Can I see the settings before I buy someone's coin?

Yes. Every Liquidity coin shows its starting cap, graduation cap and curve progress on its page and in the pool account on-chain. That's the whole point.
