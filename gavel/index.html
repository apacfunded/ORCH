In plain words

Four words, and you get it.

That is all the jargon there is.

Block

A trade big enough to move the price on its own.

Maker

A trading desk that offers you a price for your block.

Sealed

Hidden. Nobody sees any offer until the timer ends.

Slippage

The extra cost you pay because your own trade pushes the price.

How it works

Three steps. No order book.

A sheet is a short note with the coin, the amount, buy or sell, and a time limit. That is all the public sees.

Step 1

Post your sheet

Pick the coin, the amount, and whether you are buying or selling. Choose how long desks get to respond. You never write a price.

Step 2

Desks bid in secret

Makers send an offer before the timer ends. Nobody can see anyone else’s offer, so nobody can undercut by a hair.

Step 3

Best offer wins

Offers are revealed, the highest one is stamped, and your whole trade settles in a single transaction at that one price.

Pool vs sheet

Same trade. Very different bill.

Selling 1,200 SOL worth of the example coin $GOBLIN. In a pool, every slice of your order fills at a worse price than the last. On a sheet, desks price the whole block once.

FAQ

Quick answers.

What is a sealed bid?

An offer nobody else can see until the timer ends. Desks cannot copy or undercut each other, so the best honest price tends to win.

Why does it avoid the pool?

The trade settles directly with the winning desk. It never walks up the pool curve, so your own order does not move the price against you.

What if nobody bids?

The sheet expires and nothing moves. You can post it again with a longer timer or a smaller amount.
